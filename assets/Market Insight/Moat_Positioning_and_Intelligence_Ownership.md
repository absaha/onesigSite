# Moat Positioning — Where Atlas Sits in the Vertical-AI Moat Debate

**Source signal:** Linea Ventures (John Lin) LinkedIn post, Aug 2026 — "Most vertical AI SaaS is one model release away from being a feature." Three survival lanes: (1) vertical lab / own the weights (Harvey), (2) unscrapeable data + expert judgment (Mercor), (3) AI-enabled services / be the incumbent (Side, Numeral). Key comments: Ofer Razon — "be the orchestrator: own the process, not the intelligence; make the model a pluggable layer." David Jacobs (M&A advisor) — "the question for buyers becomes: what would the customer have to replace if they stopped using you?"

---

## Where Atlas sits

**Lane 2 (data) + own-the-workflow. Deliberately not lanes 1 or 3.**

**The data lane is the IP stack.** Rule packs are versioned, machine-executable regulatory content validated by experts against live UAE enforcement. The value is not the public regulation text; it is the operationalization: which rule breaks which field in which ERP, confirmed on real invoices, maintained as the regulator moves. Plus benchmark data from every teardown and the expert-confirmed question bank. This matches the M&A record: Thomson Reuters/Pagero and Vertex/ecosio paid for compliance content, certifications and coverage, not model access. Labs cannot train their way into maintained jurisdiction-specific enforcement behavior.

**The workflow lane is the record.** "Lab0 proposes; Atlas promotes" already makes extraction intelligence a pluggable layer. What is not pluggable: the decision register, lineage, owner assignments, re-run history — the accumulated memory of an engagement. A model release can replace extraction. It cannot replace two years of promoted decisions.

**Lane 3 refused on purpose:** being the incumbent means competing with our own partner channel. But ASP pricing borrows lane 3's best idea — paid per account in front of the meter (value-indexed pricing without becoming the operator).

**Lane 1 not our fight:** commodity intelligence + proprietary rules + an irreplaceable record beats owned weights in a vertical this narrow.

**One-liner:** When intelligence is a commodity, the moat is what the intelligence works on, and what it leaves behind. The rule packs are what it works on. The record is what it leaves behind.

## Customer framing — "who owns the intelligence if we leave?"

Frame ownership in three layers, and volunteer it before they ask:

| Layer | Who owns it | What happens if they leave |
|---|---|---|
| **Your record** — findings, requirements, decisions, owners, lineage, test scenarios, handoff packages | **The client/partner. Always.** Exportable in open formats at any time. | They take all of it. Every conclusion, every decision, every piece of evidence. |
| **The rule base & machinery** — rule packs, question bank, validation engine, regulation feed | **OneSig.** This is the product. | Their record stays true as of the last run. It stops being re-validated as regulations change. |
| **The models** — LLM layer | **Nobody.** Commodity, pluggable. | Irrelevant to the decision. Not the value and never was. |

**The pitch sequence:**
1. Lead with anti-lock-in: "Everything the engagement produces is yours. Export it any day, open formats, no ransom. We are not a system you get trapped in."
2. Then the distinction: "What you'd leave behind isn't your data — it's the *currency* of it. The record is a photograph of readiness on the day of the last run. Regulations move. Our whole business is keeping the rule base ahead of the regulator so your record stays alive instead of aging."
3. The Socratic version (loss framing): "If you took the last report and left today — who re-validates it when the next FTA release changes TRN behavior? Whose job is it to notice?" The answer is either "nobody" or "a team we'd have to build" — the same enablement-tax trap they already refused.
4. Close: "So the honest answer on lock-in: you own the answers, we own the question-asking machine. You can leave with every answer. You just stop getting new ones."

**Why this framing wins:** it converts the scariest procurement question (lock-in) into a demonstration of confidence, it prices the subscription against regulatory drift rather than software features, and it is *true* — which matters because tax buyers audit claims.

**Guardrails:** never disclose rule content/question bank/pack internals when explaining "the machinery." The layer table describes boundaries, not contents. Partner variant: the white-label record belongs to the partner; their client's data belongs to their client; same three-layer logic.

## Exit relevance (internal)

Jacobs's buyer question — "what would the customer have to replace if they stopped using you?" — is the diligence question the 2029 acquirer will ask. The answer we are building toward: *the maintained rule base for every live mandate, plus the installed base of living records that partners cannot walk away from without losing the memory of every engagement.* Both compound with time; neither is replaced by a model release.
