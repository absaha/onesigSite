# 3 · GTM, ICPs, and Offers — What Abhi Said

## Who the buyers are
"My buyers are **advisors, ERP integrators (system integrators), and ASPs**." He explicitly said he is not selling to the Big 4 directly: "We are selling to advisors who are below Big 4."

He asked for a doc covering: "What is the angle for each of the ICPs? Who are my top ICPs? What is the angle of messaging that we should talk about? What are my discovery questions along with them?"

## The SI/ASP economics correction (his key strategic input)
"The SI and the ERP partners [should] be similar to advisory because, for them as well, **shrinking these timelines doesn't make sense because they will lose revenue. What we should die on is how many they can take on with their current capabilities.**

Even for ASPs, I think it is similar… **ASPs are going to make money when they start transferring the invoices**. They are looking at making money in terms of invoices that are sent, so the more they get stuck with not starting the volume [the more they lose]. Also ASPs are technology houses — for them to have a bigger team doing all this enablement work before they make the money is also an angle similar to the SIs and ERP partners."

Reinforced later: "Neither the ASP nor the system integrator wants to invest in consulting resources to do this discovery work. They want a smaller, scalable team because they want to get to revenue soon." — hence: **quicker time to revenue + no new teams to spin up** must appear in SI/ASP messaging.

## The ASP commercial question
"What should be my lead, what should be my pricing, and what should be my offer for ASPs?" (Answered in `ASP_Lead_Pricing_Offer.md`: lead with the meter question; per-account banded pricing anchored to metered revenue, never per report; the longest-stuck account free as the opening offer; never revenue share; never do transmission/submission/accreditation.)

## The offer / first outreach
"Based on this, what should be my offer? If I want to create any of this, what am I really offering to the customers for them to see what all we can do? As part of the first outreach."

## The technology-vendor conversation (loss framing)
He directed that conversations with ASPs/SIs/tech vendors use consequence framing, not features: "The conversation shouldn't be 'We have AI that generates requirements.' It should be: '**What happens if your implementation starts with incomplete or incorrect assumptions?**'" He supplied the full Socratic CTO sequence (last five implementations → what arrives late → what happens downstream → revenue recognition / utilization / satisfaction over a year) and the principle: "The principle isn't to manufacture fear. It's to help someone see the full cost of an overlooked risk that they already face." He asked for the advisory one-pager recreated in this loss ideology plus a new technology-partner (SI + ASP) one-pager.

## The Dubai teardown repositioning
"What we need to bring out is that we are **productizing the problem statement** — moving away from a slide deck where there's an ever-evolving requirement… someone comes in, keeps your requirement doc locked, and walks away, and those things are never tracked. When the ASPs and the technology teams deliver, they never get the outcome on time because none of them have their piece of the bargain, and people keep blaming. We completely change that whole discovery and assessment… advisory companies just come in to give their most important time for their inputs. They don't have to do the grunt work, and thereby they are able to get more pull-through revenue." Keep it **broader than e-invoicing — any tax transformation program**.

## Outcome-staked engagement ideology
From a post he shared and adopted "in the flavor of how we do it": Finance-validated KPI locked in week 2 before build starts; a third of the fee tied to the number moving; service credit if it misses, bonus if it exceeds; the test question — "what happens to your invoice if the timeline slips?"
