## Post

OpenAI's CFO has a target for her own finance team: the zero-day close.

Not a faster monthly close. No close at all — a financial position that is continuously reconciled, so the books are simply *current*. The ritual disappears because the record never goes stale.

Now look at how tax transformation runs.

A readiness assessment is delivered in March. The deck is polished, the workshops are done, everyone signs off. Then the ERP team changes a field mapping in May. A tax decision lands in June. The regulation itself moves in July.

The deck doesn't know any of this. It says what was true in March, forever.

And the gap between what the deck says and what is actually true doesn't announce itself. It waits — for UAT, for go-live, for the authority — and it gets more expensive at every stop.

Finance is fixing this by changing what holds the truth: from documents that describe a moment to records that stay reconciled. Spending plans, actuals, and accruals in one continuously validated view, where the system flags exceptions and people decide.

Tax transformation deserves the same standard. Call it zero-day readiness:

Every requirement validated against real records, not interview notes. Every finding tied to a rule and a named owner. And when anything changes — the data, the decision, the regulation — the record re-runs and tells the owner what moved.

Not a faster assessment. No assessment at all — a readiness position that is simply current.

The firms and platforms that adopt this standard won't win because their AI is smarter. They'll win because their answer to "are we ready?" was computed this morning, not last quarter.

The deck told you what was true in March. The record tells you what is true today.
