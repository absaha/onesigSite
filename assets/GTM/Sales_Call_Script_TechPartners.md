# Sales Call Script — Technology Partners (Systems Integrators, ERP Partners & ASPs)

**OneSig Atlas · The 13-step call · Technology-partner edition**
Same spine as the advisory call, different economics. For SIs/ERP partners the currency is **build revenue and change orders**. For ASPs the currency is **the meter — live accounts transmitting invoices**. Never say "assessment" to an ASP; say readiness, validation, or enablement.

---

## Before the call

- **Intro done before the call.** Send `TechPartner_Hidden_Uncertainty_OnePager.pdf` and two lines of context with the invite. The call opens on their pipeline, not our story.
- Know: how many implementations/accounts in flight, their onboarding model, whether they've hired (or are resisting hiring) a consulting/enablement team.
- Have the twin-timeline graphic and a sample handoff package ready to share.

---

## 1 · Rapport (2 min)

Specific and short. Their accreditation news, a mandate deadline their customers face, mutual ecosystem contacts. These are operators — get to business fast; that *is* rapport here.

## 2 · Set the frame (1 min)

> "Suggestion for the next 30 minutes: I want to understand where the weeks between signing a customer and revenue actually go for you today, show you how we remove them, and agree a next step if it makes sense. If it's not a fit, we stop. Fair?"

## 3 · Clear the intent (2 min)

> "What made you take this call — is it about starting implementations faster, taking on more customers without hiring, or something else?"

Anchor on one of the three tech-partner pains: **time to revenue** (signed but not live, meter not running), **the enablement tax** (a services team they must fund before any revenue arrives), or **rework/change orders** (builds on wrong assumptions). If unclear, probe: "When a new customer signs today, how long until you're actually earning on them — and what fills that gap?"

## 4 · The play of certainties — current vs desired (10 min, the heart of the call)

**Current situation — make it concrete.** The Socratic sequence; let them build the case:

- "Say you close twice as many enterprise customers next year. What has to scale inside your implementation organization for that to work?" *(They'll say: more consultants, better onboarding, standardized delivery.)*
- "And if that doesn't scale — what becomes the bottleneck?"
- "Think about your last five implementations. How many started with everything you needed on day one?"
- "What usually arrives late?" *(Missing requirements, ERP documentation, tax decisions, master data, approvals.)*
- "When those show up late, what happens downstream?" *(Rework, waiting consultants, scope changes, slipped go-lives, angry customers.)*
- **For ASPs, add the meter question:** "Between accreditation and the first live invoice on a new account — how many weeks, and what's a stuck account worth to you per month in metered revenue you're not collecting?"

Reflect it back, sharpened:

> "So every deal you win comes with weeks of unpaid discovery, carried by people you had to hire before any build revenue arrives, working from a kickoff deck of assumptions — and when the assumptions are wrong, your consultants absorb the rework and your go-live slips. You're being squeezed on price on one side and funding a bridge team on the other."

**Desired situation — bigger than they're thinking.** They're thinking "faster onboarding." Raise it:

> "Now the other version. Every customer arrives at kickoff with validated requirements, mapped fields, identified risks, and named owners — before your team spends an hour. Build starts on day one. The same delivery team carries two or three times the accounts, because the discovery layer runs on a system instead of headcount. For an ASP: the meter starts running weeks earlier, on every account, forever. And you never had to build the services team."

**Show the gap.**

> "So the real question isn't saving two weeks on one project. If every implementation starts weeks late because discovery isn't complete — what does that mean for revenue recognition, consultant utilization, and customer satisfaction across a full year of accounts? What's that number for you?"

Make them say a number. That number is the deal.

## 5 · Transition (30 sec)

> "Want me to show you exactly how those weeks come out?"

## 6 · Intro (60 sec max)

OneSig Atlas: the readiness and validation layer that sits in front of your implementation. We find what will break, who owns the fix, and hand your team an implementation-ready package. We never touch transmission, submission, or accreditation — that's your business, not ours.

## 7 · Authority (2 min)

- #1, Tax Tech 500 e-invoicing implementation partners ranking (July 2026).
- Screen-share the handoff package sample: entity/VAT scope, source-system map, field-mapping requirements, test scenarios, remediation backlog. The artifact does the talking.
- Pattern references only — never name customers or partners.

## 8 · The set of three (5 min)

**(a) Agree the problems.** "So we've agreed: discovery eats unpaid weeks, it needs a team you don't want to fund, and wrong assumptions turn into rework and slipped go-lives. Fair?" Get the yes.

**(b) What the market is doing.** Category, never names:

> "Platform prices are compressing across the board — you're feeling that already. The vendors winning right now aren't winning on features; they're winning on time-to-live. Some of your peers are throwing bodies at onboarding and eating the margin. Others are standardizing the front end so their delivery pod runs multiples of the accounts. The ones doing neither are the ones whose customers sign and then sit — and customers who sit, churn before the meter ever runs."

**(c) The cost of not moving.**

> "Standing still means every new logo you sign makes the problem bigger, not smaller. More signed accounts stuck in the same funnel, the same team, the same weeks — and the cost isn't just the delay. It's an angry customer at UAT, discovering the gap after everything was built on it. The gap doesn't change. The moment of discovery prices it."

## 9 · The mechanism + certainty check (5 min)

> "Here's the flow. When a customer signs, they get a drop point for what they already have — invoice samples, ERP extracts, master data, tax codes, even emails and call notes. Atlas validates all of it against the jurisdiction's rules: every finding tied to a rule ID, severity Blocker/High/Flag, a named owner, a required fix. Out the other side comes your implementation-ready package — validated requirements, field mappings, test scenarios, remediation backlog. Your team starts building on day one instead of discovering on week one. When anything changes, it re-runs. No consultants hired, no workshops run."

Certainty check — mandatory:

> "Do you think that would work with your onboarding flow? What would break?"

Resolve objections here. Get the stated yes on the mechanism.

## 10 · ROI (3 min)

Their metrics, their math, live:

- **SI/ERP:** consultant utilization up (no idle time waiting on documents), build revenue recognized weeks earlier per project, fewer change orders per build, more implementations per delivery team — no discovery hires. "Take your average project value and your current signed-to-build gap. Multiply the recovered weeks across next year's pipeline."
- **ASP:** weeks-to-first-invoice down on every account → the meter starts earlier on all of them. "Stuck accounts × metered revenue per account per month × weeks recovered. That's the number this decision is about." Plus: the enablement team you never have to hire.

## 11 · Deliverables — tied to the problems (2 min)

| They said | They get |
|---|---|
| "Weeks disappear between signature and revenue" | Validated-start package: requirements, mappings, risks, owners — before kickoff |
| "We'd have to hire a services team" | The enablement layer runs on Atlas; your delivery pod stays lean |
| "Wrong assumptions become rework" | Every requirement validated against the customer's real records, tied to a rule ID |
| "Go-lives slip, customers get angry" | Test scenarios and a remediation backlog before build starts; re-runs when anything changes |
| "We can't take on more without headcount" | Same team, multiple times the accounts — capacity grows with the system, not the payroll |

## 12–13 · Price + guarantee

Only after mechanism-yes and their ROI number. Price per account/per implementation, anchored against their stated number — never against hours or reports. *(State verbally; never leave printed pricing.)*

The risk reversal:

> "Here's how sure we are. Give us your longest-stuck account — the one that's been sitting since signature. We run it free. If your team doesn't start building [or: if the meter doesn't start running] materially sooner, we part as friends and you keep the package."

Then convert: "And if it works, we do the next eight accounts sitting in front of the meter this quarter." Close with names and a date: which account, who sends the data, review call booked now.

## Guardrails (non-negotiable)

- **Never say "assessment" to ASPs.** Readiness, validation, enablement, handoff.
- We never do transmission, submission, or accreditation — say it early; it kills the competitor suspicion.
- Time-to-revenue and "no new teams to spin up" must both appear in every SI/ASP pitch.
- Never name competitors, customers, or partners. Never claim US implementations until real.
- Never disclose rule content, question bank, or pack internals.
- Loss framing surfaces real costs they already carry — never manufactured fear.
- Never revenue share; bounty/fee-at-risk variants need founder sign-off (Abhi + Kanishka) first.
