# Sales Call Script — Tax Advisory & CA Firms

**OneSig Atlas · The 13-step call · Advisory edition**
Use this to walk every advisory/CA firm call. The sequence is fixed; the words flex. Do not skip a certainty checkpoint — each step earns the right to the next.

---

## Before the call (steps you never do live)

- **Intro is done before the call.** Send the one-pager (`Advisory_Readiness_Question_OnePager.pdf`) and a two-line "who we are" in the confirmation email. Doing intros on the call "sounds weird and looks weird" — the call opens on *them*, not us.
- Know their firm: service lines, size, whether they've published anything on e-invoicing/UAE mandate, who else from their market is moving.
- Have the free-teardown offer loaded and one anonymized sample report ready to screen-share.

---

## 1 · Rapport (2–3 min)

Warm, specific, brief. Reference something real — their recent post, a mutual contact, the mandate deadline pressure in their market. Do not stretch this; senior partners read padding as junior.

## 2 · Set the frame (1 min)

You run the call, kindly. Say it out loud:

> "Here's how I'd suggest we use the next 30 minutes: I want to understand how readiness work runs in your firm today, then show you what we've built and how firms like yours use it, and if it makes sense, we'll agree a concrete next step. If at any point it's not a fit, say so and we'll stop. Fair?"

Getting "fair" = the first yes.

## 3 · Clear the intent (2 min)

Find out what they actually joined for. Without a problem to target, the call drifts.

> "Before I show anything — what made you take this call? What's the situation you're hoping this solves?"

Listen for one of the three advisory pains: **capacity ceiling** (work they can't take on), **grunt-work economics** (partner hours burned on collection/formatting), or **signature risk** (signing readiness opinions on assumptions). Anchor the whole call on the one they name. If they name none, probe: "Is this about winning more readiness work, delivering it with less senior time, or being more confident in what you sign?"

## 4 · The play of certainties — current vs desired (10 min, the heart of the call)

**Current situation — make it concrete and make it hurt (logically and emotionally).**

Socratic, never accusatory. Use the discovery set:

- "Of your last five readiness reviews, how many started with everything you needed on day one?" *(Answer is almost always none.)*
- "What usually arrives late — master data, ERP extracts, tax decisions? And who absorbs the cost when it does?"
- "How much partner and manager time goes into collecting, chasing, and formatting versus actual judgment?"
- "When the mandate wave hits and every client calls in the same quarter — how many engagements can you actually carry at once, with the team you have?"
- "How would you know a client is *ready*, rather than *believes* they're ready?"

Let them describe it. Their words, not yours. Then reflect it back worse than they said it — truthfully:

> "So every engagement starts from zero, the method lives in two people's heads, your best people spend most of the fee doing collection work, and the opinion that goes out carries your signature on assumptions nobody validated. And that's *before* the busy season."

**Desired situation — make it bigger than they were thinking.**

They're thinking "faster reports." Raise it:

> "Now imagine the other version. The same partner and manager carry three readiness engagements at once instead of one. Every scan runs the same way whoever on your team runs it. Engagement ten starts where engagement nine ended because the knowledge lives in a record, not an inbox. You sign with evidence instead of interviews. And every gap the scan finds is follow-on advisory work that stays yours."

**Show the gap.**

> "So the gap isn't 'a bit faster.' It's the difference between a firm bounded by a handful of experts' bandwidth and a firm whose capacity follows its pipeline. What do you think that gap is worth over the next two years of this mandate?"

Get a number or at least a "significant." The bigger the gap they admit, the easier everything after.

## 5 · Transition to pitch (30 sec)

> "Would it be useful if I showed you exactly how we close that gap?"

Wait for the yes.

## 6 · Intro (60 sec, only if the pre-call intro didn't land)

Two sentences maximum: OneSig Atlas is the AI-native readiness system for tax transformation. UAE e-invoicing live today; France built; the method extends to any tax transformation program.

## 7 · Authority (2 min)

- #1 in the Tax Tech 500 e-invoicing implementation partners ranking (July 2026).
- Show, don't tell: screen-share the sample readiness report and one graphic (pyramid→tower or the ceiling chart). The artifact is the authority.
- Reference the pattern, never names: "The firms we work with…" *(Never name clients or partners. Never claim US implementations until one is real.)*

## 8 · The set of three (5 min)

**(a) The problems — get agreement first.** Recap the pains *they* named in step 4: "So we agreed: engagements start incomplete, senior time is consumed by low-value work, and the opinion rests on assumptions. Fair summary?" — get the yes.

**(b) What the market is doing.** Never name competitor firms; use the category:

> "Here's what's already happening in your market. Lean specialist firms are running readiness on systems, not spreadsheets — same-size teams carrying two and three times the engagements, walking into pitches with validated findings instead of proposals for workshops. The big networks are productizing assessments too. The firms still selling manual discovery are being compared, on price, against firms selling evidence."

**(c) The cost of not moving.**

> "And the cost of standing still isn't neutral. Every readiness engagement you decline or refer out is fee revenue plus the follow-on advisory attached to it. Every opinion signed on assumptions is your name against a gap someone else will find — in UAT, or worse, the authority finds it. The gap doesn't change. The moment of discovery prices it."

## 9 · The mechanism + certainty check (5 min)

Walk the procedure, concretely:

> "Here's how it runs. Your client drops what they already have — invoice samples, ERP extracts, master data, policies, even emails. Atlas validates it against the rule base for their jurisdiction: every finding tied to a rule ID, marked CONFIRMED, REPORTED, or GAP, severity Blocker/High/Flag, each with a named owner and required fix. You review, your experts decide, your brand goes on the report. When anything changes, we re-run — the record stays current instead of dying in a deck. Your people do judgment; the engine does collection, checking, and formatting."

Then the certainty question — do not skip it:

> "Do you think that would work in your practice? Where would it break?"

Handle what they raise. You need their stated yes on the mechanism before ROI means anything.

## 10 · ROI (3 min)

Frame in their economics — throughput, never timeline compression (their fees are the timeline):

- Same partner/manager pair carries multiple engagements → more fee revenue on the same payroll.
- Grunt-work hours come off the cost base while the fee holds.
- Every scan surfaces remediation and advisory work that stays theirs — the scan is a pipeline generator, not just a deliverable.
- Do the math live with *their* numbers: "If a readiness engagement is worth X to you and you could carry two more per quarter — what's that over the mandate wave?"

## 11 · Deliverables — tied to the problems (2 min)

Relate every deliverable to a problem they agreed to:

| They said | They get |
|---|---|
| "Engagements start incomplete" | Evidence-based intake: findings from the client's real records, day one |
| "Senior time burned on grunt work" | The engine runs collection, validation, formatting; expert time goes to decisions only |
| "Opinions on assumptions" | A findings report where every line has a rule ID, evidence, severity, and owner — signed with evidence |
| "Knowledge walks out the door" | A living record that compounds engagement to engagement |
| "It's ours or nothing" | Your brand on everything; the client relationship and follow-on stays yours |

## 12–13 · Price + guarantee

Drop price only after mechanism-yes and ROI. Anchor against the gap, not the deliverable. *(Use current approved bands — never leave printed pricing behind; state it verbally.)*

The guarantee is the risk reversal we already run:

> "And here's how sure we are: pick one client — ideally the one you're least certain is ready. We'll run the teardown free, under your brand, no call required. If the findings don't change what you were about to sign, walk away and keep the report."

Close on a named next step with a date: which client, who sends the data, review call booked before you hang up.

---

## Guardrails (non-negotiable)

- Elevation, never elimination — punch the ritual, never the people. No "killing" language.
- "With Atlas," never "after AI."
- Never name competitors, clients, or partners. Never claim US implementations until real.
- Never disclose rule content, question bank, or pack internals — outputs and workflow only.
- Loss framing means helping them see a real cost they already carry — never manufactured fear.
- Fee-at-risk/guarantee variants beyond the free teardown need founder sign-off (Abhi + Kanishka) first.
