*The working sales doc · July 2026 (outcome-selling section added Aug 2026) · One page per ICP: who they are, the angle, the lead lines, and what to ask. Discovery questions map to the Qualify stage (pain, three whys, current state, affected people, metrics, urgency, champion, economic buyer).*

## 0 · The ranking (who we sell to, in order, and why now)

1. **UAE/GCC tax advisory and CA firms** — the Sept rush is live, the mandate calendar is unforgiving, and for them the scan IS revenue. Fastest close, shortest cycle.
2. **UAE ASPs (42 on the MoF register)** — every non-ready account in their pipeline is a client they cannot onboard yet. High volume, clear trigger (accreditation deadlines).
3. **SIs and ERP partners (GCC + US)** — the Pierian archetype. Bigger deals, longer cycles, the strongest expansion story (tax engine, S/4HANA, Pillar Two).
4. **Multi-country GCC advisory firms with delivery centers** — the MMJS archetype. One logo = many engagements; needs the senior-hours framing, not the headcount framing.
5. **US tax transformation firms (emerging)** — via warm intros only for now. The wedge is ERP/tax-engine fit-gap + Pillar Two, not e-invoicing.

**Not an ICP for outreach:** enterprises. They are the end client, served through the partners above. One quiet routing line on the site; no outbound.

## 1 · ICP: UAE/GCC tax advisory & CA firms

**Profile:** 10–200 people, founder/partner-led, strong client trust, thin tech bench. Trigger events: client asks about e-invoicing they can't answer at depth; a competitor publishes a readiness offering; the Sept/Oct deadline wave.
**Persona:** managing partner or head of tax (usually champion AND economic buyer in one person — small-deal version of the 5 stages, 2 meetings not 30).

**The angle (money story: the scan IS revenue, white-label credibility):**
Run Big 4-grade readiness without Big 4 overhead. The scan is a productized service your whole team can sell and deliver; the report carries your brand; the follow-on advisory, remediation, and filing work stays yours. Their fear is being out-credentialed; their win is the brandable report.

**Lead lines:** "Your clients need e-invoicing answers faster than your firm can credibly staff them." · "Interviews tell you what people believe. Your client's data tells you what breaks." · "Every scan runs the same way, whoever on your team runs it."

**Discovery questions:**
1. When a client asks about the mandate today, who in the firm answers, and what do they hand over? *(current state)*
2. How many of your clients fall in scope in the next 12 months? Have you told them yet? *(pain + metric)*
3. What would you charge for a readiness assessment today if you could deliver one credibly? *(value anchor; sets their price, not ours)*
4. Who did the last one, and how many hours did it take? *(the grunt-work number)*
5. If a Big 4 firm pitched your top client a readiness review next month, what happens? *(why now, fear activation)*
6. Who besides you would touch this — anyone whose workflow changes? *(affected parties)*
7. If the pilot report lands well with one client, what has to be true for this to become a standard service line? *(buying process + champion test)*
8. Is this decision yours alone, or does a partner group sign off? *(economic buyer)*

## 2 · ICP: UAE ASPs

**Profile:** the 42 accredited providers; sales-led, transmission-focused, differentiation-starved (39 look-alikes). Trigger events: pipeline full of not-ready accounts; accreditation/connection deadlines; a competitor moving up-stack.
**Persona:** founder/CEO or head of sales.

**The angle (money story: the meter only runs on live accounts, and you're a technology house):**
ASPs are metered businesses: revenue starts when invoices flow, not when contracts sign. Every account stuck in enablement is metering revenue deferred, and every month of "getting them ready" is a month the platform earns nothing on that logo. Worse, the enablement work in front of the meter (readiness, master-data fixes, mapping) pushes a technology house toward building a services team it never wanted. Atlas is that layer: accounts reach first live invoice faster AND in parallel, with the team you already have. The question to die on: how many accounts can you get flowing per quarter with your current people?

**Lead lines:** "You get paid when invoices move. How many accounts are sitting in front of the meter?" · "The invoice will be perfect. It still won't arrive." · "Don't build a services team to feed your platform. We are that layer, under your brand."

**Discovery questions:**
1. Of the accounts in your pipeline right now, how many are stuck at not-ready, and what's stalling them? *(pain + metric)*
2. How many accounts can your onboarding team carry in parallel today? What happens to the ones beyond that number? *(the throughput ceiling — the core question)*
3. How long is your average onboarding, signature to first live invoice, and what is an average account's monthly metered revenue once live? *(the deferred-revenue math: stuck accounts × months × per-account revenue)*
4. What happens today when a prospect's data can't connect: who fixes it, and who pays for that time? *(current state; the accidental services team)*
5. When the October deadline compresses everything into six weeks, what breaks first: sales capacity or enablement capacity? *(why now)*
6. Who owns onboarding numbers: you, a sales head, an ops head? *(economic buyer + affected parties)*
7. If your current team could carry three times the accounts in parallel, would the constraint move to sales, or is the pipeline already there? *(sizes the prize)*

## 3 · ICP: SIs & ERP partners (Pierian archetype)

**Profile:** implementation-led firms attached to S/4HANA, Oracle, Vertex/ONESOURCE/Avalara programs; assessment-then-build motion; margin lives in execution. Triggers: a program in pre-blueprint; an assessment their sellers dread pitching; change-order friction with a client.
**The angle to die on: throughput, never timeline compression.** A time-and-materials firm hears "we shorten your assessment" as "we shrink your billable weeks." Never sell the shorter engagement. Sell how many they can carry: the same partner and manager run several assessments at once instead of one, each converts to a build, and revenue per partner goes up. The engagement can even keep its price; what changes is how many of them the firm can take on with current capability.

**Personas — three doors into the same firm, same throughput story told three ways:**

- **Sales leader:** "More at-bats. Your team can only scope what delivery can staff; when the same team carries five assessments, you stop rationing proposals. And the client stops waiting a quarter to see evidence." (Gartner-backed if needed: buyers spend ~17% of the journey with sellers.)
- **Practice/COE lead (elevation, never elimination):** "Your partner and manager run more engagements; the collection, extraction, and tracking become an engine; your experts spend their hours on judgment. Same rigor, same fee if you want it, evidence instead of interviews." Never say "you don't need your analysts."
- **CEO/CFO (bench economics):** "Below the Big 4, bench doesn't exist. You either carry people the pipeline can't feed or decline work you could win. Capacity that scales with pipeline, not headcount — grow revenue per partner without growing payroll."

**Shared lead lines:** "How many assessments can your current team carry this quarter? That number is your growth ceiling." · "We don't shorten your engagement. We multiply how many you can run." · "Walk in with the problems already found. Win the build." · "Concrete requirements in, fewer change orders out."

**Discovery questions:**
1. Walk me through your current assessment: how many weeks, staffed how, priced how? *(current state; the pyramid)*
2. How many assessments did the practice run last year, and how many did you decline, delay, or under-scope because of staffing? *(the throughput ceiling — the core question)*
3. What percentage of your assessments convert to builds, and how long is the gap between the two? *(time-to-revenue metric)*
4. Pull up your last implementation: how many change requests, and how many trace back to discovery? *(scope-creep number)*
5. When pipeline spikes, what do you do: hire, subcontract, or decline? What did that cost last year? *(bench economics; CEO door)*
6. Who owns the assessment methodology internally, and how will they feel about this? *(the practice-leader landmine, found early)*
7. Who would sign a per-engagement license: practice, sales, or corporate? *(economic buyer)*
8. If we ran one teardown on a live pre-blueprint account, what would it need to show for you to put it in front of the program director? *(pilot criteria; sets up the Align stage)*

## 4 · ICP: Multi-country GCC advisory firms (MMJS archetype)

**Profile:** 100+ people, GCC footprint, offshore delivery center, own technology ambitions, sector GTM (seminars, federations). Triggers: clients asking about the Deloitte-style "hybrid model"; seminar season; delivery center utilization pressure.
**Persona:** managing partner/group CEO; secondary: technology practice head (watch the build-it-ourselves reflex).

**The angle (senior hours, standardization, sector campaigns):**
Even with a strong delivery center, partner and senior-manager hours are the wall. Atlas makes every scan run the same way whoever operates it: a partner in Dubai or the delivery center offshore. And the teardown turns their seminar motion into proof: one member company's real data, torn down, becomes the centerpiece of the next sector event. Support till the last mile, on the record.

**Lead lines:** "The Deloitte pitch your clients are reading is 'global core, local execution.' We're the version with machinery, under your brand." · "Your delivery center can deliver partner-grade readiness, provably." · "One teardown per sector seminar. Proof instead of slides."

**Discovery questions:**
1. When your clients bring you the Big 4's hybrid-model narrative, what's your answer today? *(why you; competitive frame)*
2. Which three sectors carry your practice, and what's your event calendar for them this half? *(the seminar play)*
3. How does an engagement flow between Dubai and your delivery center today, and where does partner review become the bottleneck? *(senior-hours pain)*
4. What has your technology team built or bought for this already? *(build-reflex check; position as complement)*
5. What would make readiness a firm-standard service across all offices rather than one partner's specialty? *(standardization; scale of deal)*
6. Who decides on a firm-wide platform: you alone, an exco, country partners? *(economic buyer + buying process)*
7. Which single client, if we tore down one account free, would create the loudest internal proof? *(pilot selection)*

## 6 · Discovery discipline (applies to every ICP)

- Every first call must leave with: the most painful problem named, a number attached to it, why-now confirmed or disconfirmed, a champion hypothesis, and the economic buyer's name. If you can't fill those five, the call isn't done.
- **The champion test:** ask them to introduce you to someone (a colleague, a client for the teardown). If they won't, keep looking.
- **The teardown is the pilot** — before running it, agree what it must show and set the date you'll discuss the outcome. Never run a free teardown without a scheduled readout.
- Log every answer as a conversation connector: their exact words about pain go back into the next prospect's outreach.
- Price never appears before the teardown is delivered. The floor is $10k; the Association contact and all public materials never hear a smaller number.

## 7 · Outcome-selling discipline (applies to every ICP)

*Source: "What Outcome-Selling Really Means for Consulting Firms" (Florian Heinrichs, The Visible Authority, Sep 2025). Adopted Aug 2026 as a standing frame for all ICP messaging — ours AND the story we hand partners to use with their clients.*

**The core rule: lead with the outcome, never the capability.** "We implement SAP" / "we integrate AI" / "we have an AI discovery platform" makes the buyer do the work of connecting your expertise to their world — that's what commoditizes the pitch and eats pricing power. Every lead line in this doc must name the business result, not the machinery. Test any new line against the article's pattern: "We use AI to reduce IT's time-to-ticket resolution without adding headcount" — specific outcome, named constraint, no category-speak.

**Outcome-selling ≠ guarantees.** It is focus and commercial clarity, not control. We don't run the partner's firm, and the partner doesn't run the client's. So the promise is never "20% lower costs in 6 months, or your money back" — it's a strategic choice about *which single outcome we exist to serve* and enough repetition to speak about it with confidence. This also protects the sell: no overclaiming to skeptics (consistent with the S2 never-overclaim rule and [[feedback_sales_call_habits]]).

**The one outcome per ICP (the result each pitch is organized around):**

| ICP | The outcome we sell |
|---|---|
| UAE/GCC advisory & CA firms | A readiness revenue line the whole firm can sell and deliver — without hiring a tech bench |
| UAE ASPs | More accounts reaching first live invoice per quarter, with the team they already have |
| SIs & ERP partners | More assessments per partner and fewer change orders per build — revenue per partner grows, payroll doesn't |
| Multi-country GCC advisory (MMJS) | Partner-grade output from the delivery center, provably, on every engagement |
| US transformation firms (emerging) | Discovery cost-to-deliver drops while Pillar Two / engine data readiness becomes a sellable outcome |

**Repetition is what earns the outcome language — and Atlas IS the repetition engine.** The article's hardest truth: you can't declare an outcome strategy, you earn it through similar work, similar clients, similar problems, codified until variance drops. That's structurally what Atlas does — every scan runs the same way whoever runs it, every engagement's numbers accumulate in the record. Two uses: (1) for OUR pitch, the engagement history is what will eventually let us say "firms like yours typically see X" and show the receipts; (2) for the PARTNER pitch, this is a first-class benefit — Atlas hands a firm the codified delivery pattern and baseline data that outcome-selling requires, without a decade of manual repetition. A partner using Atlas can move from selling capacity ("a team of five for six months") to selling a result with evidence behind it. That shift — vendor to business partner — is itself part of what we sell.

**Two traps the article names (both live for us):**
- **Outcome-based proposition ≠ outcome-based pricing.** Pricing on outcomes transfers financial risk and is only safe once variance is low across many similar engagements. This is exactly why the fee-at-risk terms in the outcome-staked model stay unconfirmed until the founders sign off — and why we never open with them. Sell the outcome frame now; earn the outcome pricing later.
- **The value-based-pricing / customization trap.** The moment every engagement becomes "special," repeatability dies: harder scoping, improvised delivery, rising variance, trust that depends on one senior person. For us: protect the standard scan SKU — customization requests route to scope tiers, never to bespoke delivery. For partners: this is another reason the vault/pack architecture matters — their methodology gets codified once and enforced everywhere, instead of reinvented per engagement.

**Anti-sprawl rule:** each new service line resets the outcome-credibility clock. Before adding an offer, ask: does it deepen the one outcome this ICP buys, or start a new learning curve? (Consistent with the 90-day scope freeze discipline.)

## 8 · Video scripts — technology partners with a consulting arm (Aug 2026)

### 8.1 · The Zimmer Biomet cold open (optional hook — strongest available)

> Zimmer Biomet paid Deloitte sixty-nine million dollars to consolidate nine ERP systems onto S/4HANA. Fifty-one change orders later, the fees hit ninety-two million — and the go-live was so broken the company couldn't ship product or generate an invoice for months. They're now suing for a hundred and seventy-two million.
>
> Fifty-one change orders is the tell. That's not a technology problem. That's nobody agreeing on how the business actually runs — before go-live.

*Sourcing: lawsuit filed Sept 2025, publicly reported (MassDevice, Loeb & Loeb, UpperEdge, Third Stage/Kimberling post-mortem). $69M/$92M/51-change-order figures are from the complaint — say "according to the complaint" if pressed. The "51 change orders is the tell" framing is adapted from a public LinkedIn comment (Hillel Shalev) — paraphrase, don't quote with attribution.*

*Two more captures from the same thread, for calls and long-form:*
- **The optimism-inheritance point** (adapted from Steve Hourigan's comment): go-live slipped five times; hundreds of people — UAT testers, order-to-cash staff, warehouse teams — knew each new date was wrong. Their knowledge traveled upward through status reports that inherited the program's optimism. The post-mortem's thirteen lessons are all governance nouns; not one says where the contradicting evidence was supposed to come from. **Our answer: from the records, not the reporting chain.** A tested map doesn't inherit optimism.
- **The AI-on-a-guess point** (from Michael Schank's post): AI can't draft an accurate charter, test plan, or status view unless it already has a trustworthy, current picture of how the organization runs. "Without that, you're not applying AI to the problem. You're applying it to a guess." — this is the tech-partner version of our discovery thesis; use with SIs pitching AI programs.
