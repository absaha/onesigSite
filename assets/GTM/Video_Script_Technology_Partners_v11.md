# Video Script — Technology Partners with a Consulting Arm
## v11 · "Operating model" arc · Aug 2026

*~3 minutes · Audience: SIs, ERP partners, tax technology vendors with consulting/delivery arms.*
*Spine (modeled on OpenAI's "finance at the speed of business" opening): universal moment cold open → diagnosis as an operating-model problem → the bigger opportunity → tagline → platform intro ("we run our own delivery on it") → what it does, stated as outcomes → governed and auditable close.*
*Sourcing note: Zimmer Biomet figures are from the public complaint (filed Sept 2025) — say "according to the complaint" if pressed. "AI-native" kept per founder's explicit choice.*

---

Every delivery leader knows this moment: the contract is signed, the kickoff deck says one thing — and three months later, discovery says another. A workshop surfaces a process nobody scoped. A data extract contradicts what the client swore in the sales cycle. And the program plan you committed to starts drifting before the build has even begun.

The problem is that too much of your firm's expertise — your most senior, most expensive people — goes into *extracting* the understanding, one workshop at a time, rather than shaping the transformation. Months of it. Manual, fragmented, non-repeatable.

That's not a project problem. That's an operating model problem for the entire delivery organization. It's why more engagements always means more analysts, and why the cost of delivery grows in a straight line with revenue.

*(beat)*

And the stakes aren't abstract. Zimmer Biomet paid sixty-nine million dollars to consolidate nine ERPs. Fifty-one change orders later, fees hit ninety-two million and the go-live couldn't produce an invoice. Fifty-one change orders is the tell: nobody agreed on how the business actually ran — before the build.

Because here's the thing: execution is solved. Two years ago, had AI ever finished an end-to-end task for you? Today it happens every day. Every firm's build works now. Every firm configures the same engines, the same ERPs. What still separates one firm from another is how well you understand the client's business before you build — and how fast.

But the opportunity is bigger than doing discovery faster. It's a delivery organization that has visibility across every engagement, sees the risk while it can still be designed out, and puts its senior people on judgment — the thing clients actually hire you for. That's what we mean by **delivery at the speed of the deal**: your firm focuses on the transformation, not the extraction.

*(beat)*

This becomes possible with Atlas. Atlas is an AI-native platform that stays with your engagement from design through go-live — it reads the client's documents, data, and ERP configuration, works a goal for hours if needed, and turns it into a finished, decision-ready work product. We run our own delivery on it.

With Atlas, your teams:

Answer most of discovery before anyone books a meeting — the agents read what the client already wrote down, and only the questions no document can answer reach a call, run by your people.

Get a living map of how tax actually runs in that client — every workflow, dependency, and gap tested against real records, so your team knows what's confirmed and what's just a claim. Implementation risk surfaces before the build, not in month four.

Design the future state on the same platform, in the same detail — so the distance between today and the destination isn't a slide. It's the program plan: every gap named, every requirement traced, every decision owned. What got designed gets tested; what got tested goes live.

And keep everything learned. Every engagement makes the record smarter about that enterprise, so the next transformation — the remediation, the tax engine, the next mandate — starts from everything the last one taught it, not from zero.

*(beat)*

Which adds up to the biggest shift of all: you stop selling capacity and start selling a result. Not "a team of five for six months" — "here's the result this engagement is built to deliver, and here's the evidence behind it." Ten engagements in, you're not saying "trust us." You're showing receipts.

And all of it is governed and auditable — every proposal reviewed, every promotion approved by a named person with a rationale, every finding traceable to its source. Nothing applies itself. Your client can inspect the chain.

*(beat)*

Transformation programs don't fail because people can't execute. They fail because the understanding never got extracted — and the destination never got designed — before the build started.

Fifty-one change orders, or a map before the build.

That's the choice now.

## Delivery notes

- Four *(beats)*: after the operating-model diagnosis · after the platform intro setup · before the outcome-selling shift · before the close.
- The open must feel like recognition, not setup — "every delivery leader knows this moment" only works if the three examples are instantly familiar. Don't rush them.
- "Delivery at the speed of the deal" is the v11 tagline; if it doesn't test well, fall back to the v10 structure without a tagline.
- "We run our own delivery on it" mirrors OpenAI's "our entire finance department uses it" — only keep this line if it's true enough to defend.
- Per-client framing on accumulated context stays deliberate — no cross-client learning claims in anything recorded.
- Governance beat is new in v11 (from the OpenAI arc) — it's one paragraph on purpose; don't let it grow.
