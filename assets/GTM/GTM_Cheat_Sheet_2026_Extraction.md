## 2 · The GTM Iceberg 2026

Everyone watches the top: website, content, demos, outbound, LinkedIn posts. **The GTM that wins is built under the waterline.**

- **Above the waterline (execution):** ads · website · landing pages · demos · outbound · LinkedIn content · community
- **What we create:** Plans, Playbooks & Alignment → Value Proposition & Offer
- **What we profile:** Buyer Personas & Process → Ideal Customer Profile
- **What we analyze:** Market Data → Customer Data → **GTM Strategy** (the base)

> Strategy is knowing who you sell to, why they care, and how they decide to buy. Skip that, and even great execution turns into random revenue.

## 3 · How to Build a Clear ICP

- **Customer data:** your best ICP is already sitting in your CRM — what industry they're in, how big the team is, what tools they use, pains they mention out loud.
- **Pain points:** read your call recordings and interviews, then find the *real* pain — money at stake, cost & ROI pressure, time they save, less risk to carry.
- **Additional data points:** you usually need ~4 to 13 data points to lock an ICP — company facts & tools, headcount & team size, buying signals, fresh funding news.
- **Vague vs sharp:** "B2B SaaS companies" = too broad, no real filter — everyone qualifies, so nobody does. "Mid-market SaaS with a sales team" = half right, still too loose. Sharp = mid-market B2B SaaS, named revenue-team size, hired an SDR/sales leader in the last 90 days (a filter with signals and a clock on it).
- **Micro-segments:** split the market into 3 to 6 groups of 100–300 companies — what do they do and for whom, how do they actually sell, are they growing or hiring, what makes them buy *now*.
- **Target list:** start with 100–300 accounts that fit your best groups; only chase your best segments; check it with sales and marketing; list what outbound can actually reach next quarter; refresh the list each quarter.
- **Questionnaire (persona):** job role and who they report to; do they start, sway, decide, or veto; who controls the budget; the numbers they get judged on; who else sits in the buying call.
- **Refine your persona:** once the account fits, decide who inside it you talk to — what is the champion trying to pull off, what is blocking them right now, what breaks if they do nothing, what does a win look like for them.

## 4 · How to Build an AI GTM Strategy (the funnel)

**Step 1 — Problem Discovery & ICP Clarity** *(know your buyers before you automate a thing)*
- How: look at your ~20 best customers and find the real pattern; read their real words — trust recordings over guesses; write one sharp ICP before you automate anything.
- Why: bad ICP in = bad pipeline out — everything after inherits it; get it right and AI personalizes at scale, not at random; the best also nail who they're **not** for, just as sharply.

**Step 2 — Validation & Message Testing** *(test hooks, angles, sequences before you scale)*
- How: send 20–50 by hand per single angle before you scale; change one thing at a time — hook, offer, or CTA; keep what gets replies, kill the rest; scale later.
- Why: small tests catch the losers early; replies come from relevance — one proven hook beats 10× the volume; use building blocks, not 200 sequences.

**Step 3 — Personalized Multichannel Outreach** *(one story, three channels, one owner per account)*
- How: go 2–3× on accounts with a live signal, stay steady on the rest; tell one story across email, LinkedIn, and ads; give each account one owner so touches line up.
- Why: multichannel/ABM materially lifts meeting rates vs one channel; warm them first — content and ads give the cold touch context; speak to the person *and the trigger*, not empty flattery.

**Step 4 — Generation & Optimization Loop** *(feed results back weekly, not quarterly)*
- Every deal you close sharpens the next round of targeting and copy; AI only compounds when you train it — weekly; good accounts throw off signals, and signals trigger your next play.

**Operationalize each layer:** wire every AI motion into the flow (research → outreach → follow-up → CRM → report); put a clear owner on every automation; give each layer one number it owns; write the play down so anyone can run it.
**Weekly AI rhythm:** check what AI produced every week, not once a quarter; audit data & deliverability on a schedule; refresh sequences, hooks, prompts every two weeks; always keep one test running.
**Close the feedback loop:** treat AI like a new hire — give it feedback; feed what won straight back into the prompts; update workflows from real results; kill what flopped fast, then double down on what compounds.

## 5 · Cowork for GTM Teams (AI-assistant plays)

| Play | What it does | Typical connectors |
|---|---|---|
| **Prospecting — ICP lead-list builder** | Build the ICP from closed-won patterns, then pull lookalike accounts + verified contacts into a list | Apollo / Clay / CRM |
| **Research — account intel brief** | One-page brief per account before a call: company, recent news, tech stack, hiring signals, likely pains | Web + enrichment tools |
| **Pipeline — follow-up & QBR trigger** | Review open pipeline, pull latest touchpoints, draft the follow-ups and flag stalled deals | HubSpot / CRM + email |
| **Close — pitch deck & demo booking** | Build a tailored deck for the account and handle the scheduling motion | Slides + calendar |
| **Review — sequence performance digest** | Pull last week's sequence data, flag winners and losers, propose next tests | Apollo / HubSpot |

## 6 · Headcount Planning

**Pre-AI sales model:** SDR/AE/ops pyramid — pricey per-seat licenses, too many overlapping tools, lots of manual work.
**AI-first sales team:** smaller team (strategist + GTM engineer + ops) — fewer licenses, AI handles research & drafts, more output per person.

Three constraints to plan around:
1. **Capacity:** how much pipeline can your SDRs really make, and how many deals can your AEs really work? Keep them in balance or you pay for idle reps or wasted pipeline.
2. **Market:** your TAM runs out — each new rep gets a smaller patch; space out touches (every few months, not every week); some markets ceiling far sooner than others.
3. **Timeline:** SDRs ramp in ~3 months, AEs ~5–7; SDR tenure ≈ 1.5 yrs with ~45% annual attrition, AE tenure ≈ 18 months with ~34% — hiring is slower and leakier than it looks.
