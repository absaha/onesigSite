# Video Script — Technology Partners with a Consulting Arm

*~3 minutes · Aug 2026 (v10, current) · Audience: SIs, ERP partners, tax technology vendors with consulting/delivery arms.*
*Spine: Zimmer Biomet cold open → execution-is-solved diagnosis → discovery is manual/fragmented/non-repeatable → platform from design through go-live → accumulated context → outcome-selling shift → callback close.*
*Sourcing note: Zimmer Biomet lawsuit publicly confirmed (filed Sept 2025; MassDevice, Loeb & Loeb, UpperEdge, Third Stage/Kimberling). $69M/$92M/51-change-order figures are from the complaint — say "according to the complaint" if pressed. "AI-native" kept per founder's explicit choice for these scripts.*

---

Zimmer Biomet paid Deloitte sixty-nine million dollars to consolidate nine ERP systems onto S/4HANA. Fifty-one change orders later, fees hit ninety-two million — and the go-live was so broken the company couldn't ship product or generate an invoice for months. Now they're suing for a hundred and seventy-two million.

Fifty-one change orders is the tell. That's not a technology problem. That's nobody agreeing on how the business actually runs — before go-live.

*(beat)*

And here's the uncomfortable part: your engagements carry the same risk, for the same reason. Because the hard part was never the build.

Think about it — two years ago, had AI ever finished an end-to-end task for you? Probably not. Today it happens every day. Execution is solved. Every firm's build works now. Every firm configures the same tax engines, the same ERPs.

Which is why every pitch sounds the same. "We implement tax engines." "We integrate ERPs." "We do AI." Those are capabilities — and a capability gives your client something to compare, not a reason to choose.

What still separates one firm from another is how well you understand the client's business before you build. And right now, that understanding is manual, fragmented, and non-repeatable — produced by your most expensive people, one workshop at a time. That's why months disappear between the signed contract and a working implementation. That's why more engagements means more analysts, and the cost center grows in a straight line with revenue.

That's the job we do — on one AI-native platform that stays with your engagement from design through go-live.

*(beat)*

It starts with what the client already wrote down. Our agents read the documents, the data, the ERP configuration — and answer most of the discovery questions before anyone books a meeting. The only questions that reach the client are the ones no document can answer. Those go on a call, run by your people.

What comes out is a living map of how tax actually runs in that client today. Every workflow, every dependency, every gap — tested against their real records, so your team knows which requirements are confirmed and which are just claims. Implementation risk surfaces here — before the build — not in month four.

But the map is only half the job. On the same platform, your consultant configures what the process *should* look like after the transformation — the future state, in the same detail as the current one. The distance between those two isn't a slide anymore. It's the program plan: every gap named, every requirement traced, every decision owned.

And the platform carries it from there — through the build, all the way to go-live. What got designed gets tested. What got tested goes live. Nobody's change order number fifty-one is hiding in an untested requirement.

*(beat)*

Here's what that means for your firm.

You run more discovery without building another discovery team. The same team onboards more customers. Your senior people spend their hours on judgment — the thing clients actually hire you for.

You hit the dates you commit to, without the churn — because the surprises that eat programs from the inside never make it in.

And here's what nobody else keeps: everything learned. Every engagement makes the record smarter about that enterprise — its systems, its data, its processes, its requirements, its decisions. So when the next transformation comes — the ERP remediation, the tax engine, the next mandate — your team doesn't start from zero. They start from everything the last one taught the record. Every engagement opens the door to the one after it, and makes it cheaper to walk through.

Which adds up to the biggest shift of all: you stop selling capacity and start selling a result. Not a guarantee — you don't run your client's business, and they know it. Clarity. Instead of "here's a team of five for six months," you say: "here's the result this engagement is built to deliver, and here's the evidence behind it."

And you can't just declare that — you have to earn it, through the same work done the same way, enough times to speak with confidence. That usually takes a decade. On our platform it's structural: every engagement runs the same codified way, every result lands in the record. Ten engagements in, you're not saying "trust us." You're saying "firms like yours typically see this" — and showing the receipts.

That's what moves you, in your client's eyes, from vendor to partner.

*(beat)*

Transformation programs don't fail because the people can't execute. They fail because the understanding never got extracted — and the destination never got designed — before the build started.

Fifty-one change orders, or a map before the build.

That's the choice now.

## Delivery notes

- Four *(beats)*: hook lands · model pivot · firm-payoff pivot · close. Pause a full second at each.
- The "two years ago" question works because the viewer answers it privately before you make your claim — don't rush it.
- Per-client framing on accumulated context ("smarter about *that* enterprise") is deliberate — cross-client learning claims spook partners on data separation. Keep it per-client in anything recorded.
