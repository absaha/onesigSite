Section 1

What UAE requires

The UAE Federal Tax Authority is moving e-invoicing onto a Peppol-AE PINT model with structured UBL 2.1 documents transmitted through pre-approved Accredited Service Providers. The ASP is the regulated transmission path. The compliance record sits in the taxpayer's stack.

The mandate covers tax invoices, credit notes, debit notes, and self-billed invoices with mandatory and conditional fields across header, parties, lines, tax, payment terms, and document references. Each field has a business meaning and a technical schema location, and most ERPs do not expose them in the same place.

  • FTA-certified ASP transmission path
  • PINT-AE / UBL 2.1 structured invoice format
  • Tax registration numbers, scheme IDs, and counterparty identifiers
  • Document type codes (TI, CN, DN, self-billed)
  • Line-level tax codes, rates, and amounts
  • Currency codes, rounding rules, and exchange-rate handling
  • References to contracts, purchase orders, and prior invoices
  • Phased rollout with clear timelines and reporting obligations
Section 2

Why source data readiness matters

The mandate fails on data, not on transmission. Most ERPs were never set up to expose what UAE PINT-AE expects: clean tax registration numbers for both parties, scheme IDs, line-level VAT codes, structured currency handling, and document type classifications.

Teams find out at submission time. The ASP returns an error. The invoice did not clear. AR has shipped, the customer has the invoice, but the regulator does not. Every one of those becomes a manual correction, a customer call, and a workpaper at month-end.

Source data readiness is the work that comes before transmission. It is the difference between a clean go-live and six months of triaging rejections.

Key idea
Mandates change faster than ERP cores should. Catch the gap in the data, not in the regulator's response.
