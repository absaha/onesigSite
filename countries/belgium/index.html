Section 1

Peppol validation is not enough

Free Peppol validators tell you whether the XML parses. They do not tell you whether the invoice is correct. They do not catch the things that will get the document rejected by the buyer's AP, the regulator, or the ERP on the other side.

Belgium adds a layer of country-specific rules on top of the generic Peppol model: enterprise number formatting, structured payment means, VAT regime checks, document-type expectations, and buyer rule packs that vary by counterparty.

Two practitioner diagnoses already public this year illustrate the gap: PaymentMeansCode misuse leading to duplicate-payment risk, and XML-PDF fidelity failures hiding behind a passing validator. The XML parses. The transaction is wrong.

Reality check
Validation is the floor. Correctness is the product.
Section 2

Belgian enterprise number and endpoint readiness

Every Belgian counterparty needs a clean BCE/KBO enterprise number, a valid Peppol participant identifier, and a properly registered endpoint and scheme ID. Most teams discover the gaps in production.

  • BCE / KBO enterprise number formatting and validity
  • Peppol participant identifier (iso6523-actorid-upis::0208:...)
  • Endpoint scheme ID (electronicAddressIdentifier) registration
  • SMP / AP discoverability across the Belgian network
  • Document type and process identifier alignment with Belgian profiles
  • Structured Belgian address fields (street, postcode, locality)
Section 3

Counterparty readiness

Even if your invoice is perfect, it does not clear if your counterparty is not actually reachable on Peppol. A counterparty readiness sweep is the unglamorous work that prevents the first month of go-live from being a backlog of bounced documents.

  • Are your customers and suppliers discoverable on Peppol?
  • Are they registered to receive the document type you are sending?
  • Are they mapped to a valid AP that accepts structured documents?
  • Are their endpoint scheme IDs current and verifiable?
  • Which counterparties are at risk and need an alternate path?
  • Ongoing monitoring as counterparties update or churn endpoints
Section 4

Smart validation and fix suggestions

OneSig does not stop at "rule failed." Every validation error becomes a business fix tied to a specific source field, with a recommended remediation.

What the validator says
What OneSig says
PEPPOL-EN16931-R004: tax amount mismatch.

Line 3 rounds €0.01 below the invoice total. Change rounding mode to half-up and update the source ERP tax calculation.

UBL-CR-431: Document type not allowed.

Counterparty 0208:0123456789 is not registered to receive credit notes. Re-route via secondary AP or send corrective debit memo.

BR-CL-25: Payment means code invalid.

PaymentMeansCode 31 was used for a SEPA credit transfer. Switch to 30 to prevent duplicate-payment risk on the AP side.

Schematron: enterprise number invalid.

BE0123456789 does not match BCE format BE0NNNNNNNNN. Reformat in customer master or block at submission.

Section 5

ERP writeback

Most Peppol stacks submit and stop. OneSig closes the loop. Validation status, rejection reason, clearance reference, lifecycle status, and the link to the evidence pack flow back into the ERP, so AR, AP, treasury, and tax all read the same record.

  • Validation status (passed / failed / under review)
  • Rejection reason and field-level cause
  • Clearance / receipt reference
  • Lifecycle status (sent, delivered, accepted, corrected)
  • Evidence pack link
  • Correction history
  • Timestamp trail
Why writeback matters
Without it, every downstream team rebuilds the same status from scratch - and rebuilds it wrong.
